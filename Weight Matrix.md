## Design 2

|                                                                | Weight | Score | Why |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ | ----- | --- |
| **Clarity of Differential Demonstration**                      | 0.20   | 3     | The two independently controlled belts can create different speeds at the left and right wheels. This allows students to observe how an axle differential accommodates a speed difference between its outputs. However, the vehicle is held stationary and does not follow an actual curved path. Therefore, students see a simulated wheel-speed difference rather than a complete demonstration of how the vehicle and its wheels move during a real turn. |
| **Clarity of Transfer Case Demonstration**                     | 0.20   | 3     | The selector can show how the drivetrain connections change between modes. However, each belt contacts both the front and rear wheels on the same side. With similar wheel sizes and little wheel-to-belt slip, the average front and rear axle speeds will be approximately equal. As a result, the current bench cannot clearly reproduce the front-to-rear speed difference needed to compare an open centre differential with a locked front-to-rear connection during a turn. In addition, the belts can rotate wheels that are not being powered through the transfer case, making the power path less obvious to students. |
| **Lab usability (setup + test time, all 4 students involved)** | 0.15   | 5     | The preassembled belt bench could reduce setup time because students would not need to build a separate road course before each test. Once the vehicle is positioned, the belts and controls are checked, and the power is connected, students can use the separate speed controls and selector lever to change the test conditions. The stationary setup also allows all four students to gather around the model: they can take turns operating the controls, observing wheel and differential motion, and recording results. |
| **Safety**                                                     | 0.14   | 3     | The concept illustration shows exposed moving belts, rollers and wheels. The locations where the belts enter the rollers and where the wheels contact the belts can create pinch points during operation. Students may also reach toward the moving mechanism while trying to observe or adjust it. Additional guards are therefore needed around hazardous interfaces, while transparent sections should keep the drivetrain visible. The motor-driven bench should also have easily accessible stop controls outside the guarded area. |
| **Serviceability (Ease of repair)**                            | 0.10   | 2     | Compared with a simpler demonstration setup, Design 2 has more components that may need inspection or replacement, including two belts, rollers, motors, controllers and wiring. A worn or damaged belt may require partial disassembly of the bench, followed by replacement, alignment and tension adjustment. Electrical faults may also need troubleshooting in the motors, controllers or wiring. If these components are placed under the vehicle or inside the frame, access for repair could be more difficult. |
| **Ease of storage/transport**<br>                              | 0.08   | 3     | The vehicle, two belt assemblies, rollers, motors and supporting frame must all be stored or transported together as part of the demonstration setup. This may make the complete assembly bulkier and less convenient to carry than a smaller, simpler model. During transport, the belts and rollers could also move out of alignment, while the controls, wiring and transparent covers may need protection. The actual storage size and weight still depend on the final design. |
| **Cost margin below $300**                                     | 0.06   | 1     | In addition to the vehicle and drivetrain, Design 2 requires two motors, separate speed controllers, belts, rollers, a supporting frame and protective guards. These additional mechanical and electrical components increase the expected cost and may leave little margin below the CAD $300 limit. If any parts need to be replaced or additional safety components are required, the cost could increase further. A complete bill of materials with supplier prices is needed before the final cost can be confirmed. |
| **Easy to manufacture**                                        | 0.05   | 1     | The team must build the vehicle and drivetrain as well as a separate belt bench with a frame, rollers, motor mounts and guards. The two belt systems need to be assembled with suitable alignment and tension so that the belts run consistently and remain in contact with the wheels. The motors, controllers and power supply also need to be installed, wired and tested. These additional mechanical and electrical tasks make the design more complex and time-consuming to manufacture and assemble. |
| **Future Proof (Ease of future additions)**                    | 0.02   | 3     | The independent belt controls provide some flexibility for changing left and right wheel speeds, and additional sensors or visual markers could potentially be added. However, the current frame and two-belt layout limit the types of conditions that can be demonstrated. For example, independently creating different front and rear wheel speeds would likely require changes to the belt arrangement, additional drive components and revised controls. Future additions may therefore be possible, but some would require substantial redesign. |
| **Total**                                                      | 1      | 2.98  |     |
